## 1. Introduction

A reimplementation earns trust by being compared with the thing it reimplements. For a splice-site
predictor the interesting comparison is not per-position accuracy on a held-out test set — that is
reported in the OpenSpliceAI paper [2] — but agreement on the quantity people actually act on: the delta
score assigned to a variant.

That comparison is available in principle, because SpliceAI ships a precomputed collection of genic
SNV scores across the human genome. Here, **genome-wide** describes the collection's genomic extent;
the collection does not include every genomic base or every possible GRCh38 SNV. Coverage percentages
refer to this published collection. The comparison is hard in practice for three reasons. The published scores are
**masked**, so an unmasked re-scoring is not comparable. They are computed against a **specific gene
annotation**, so a re-scoring against a different one measures the annotations as much as the models. And
there are **3.4 billion of them**, which is too many to hold in a table and enough that a silent failure
in the middle of a month-long job is entirely plausible.

This report is about meeting those three requirements. Section 2 covers the scorer, section 3 the scoring
campaign, section 4 the analysis pipeline, and section 5 the results. Sections 3 and 4 include what went
wrong, because the failures were more instructive than the successes and two of them would recur for
anyone attempting the same thing.

### What this study can and cannot establish

It is a **concordance** study with one annotation-anchored extension. SpliceAI is the comparator, not
ground truth: no experimentally validated splicing outcome enters the analysis. Section 5.7 anchors
variants to annotated splice sites, which bounds the interpretation of a disagreement but does not settle
it — a call far from any annotated site may be a genuine unannotated site rather than an error. Nothing
here ranks the two predictors on biological accuracy.

## 2. Scoring a variant, and scoring three billion of them

### 2.1 What a delta score is

For a variant, OpenSpliceAI and SpliceAI both compute four numbers: the change in the model's predicted
probability that a position is used as a splice acceptor or donor, decomposed into gain and loss. Each is
accompanied by the position at which that change is largest, within a window of `-D` to `+D` bases. The
model is evaluated twice — once on the reference sequence and once on the alternate — and the delta scores
are differences of those two predictions.

Two properties of that definition matter later. First, the four scores are **not independent**: they are
derived from the same pair of forward passes, so an implementation difference propagates into all four.
Second, the maximum of the four is what a caller thresholds, so it is the quantity whose agreement matters
operationally even though it is the least mechanistically interpretable.

### 2.2 Masking

With `-M 1` a score is suppressed unless it is consistent with the annotation: a **gain** is zeroed when
the position of the predicted gain coincides with an already-annotated splice site, and a **loss** is
zeroed when it does *not*. The published SpliceAI scores are masked this way, so the re-scoring must be
too.

The precise rule is worth stating because section 5.7 depends on it. For each variant the scorer computes
the offset to the nearest annotated exon boundary, pooling acceptor and donor boundaries together, and
masks on whether the predicted position equals that offset. The consequence is that under masking a
**loss score is retained only when its selected position matches the nearest per-gene
exon boundary**, including transcript ends. A gain score is suppressed when its selected
position matches that boundary. That asymmetry in what the two kinds of score are even allowed to say turns
out to predict which of them the two predictors agree about.

### 2.3 The batched, reference-deduplicated scorer

Scored naively, each variant costs two forward passes over a {{study.flanking_size|,d}}-nucleotide
context. Two observations make the campaign tractable. Variants at the same position share a reference
pass, and within a chunk many variants fall in the same gene, so the reference computation is
**deduplicated within a position** rather than repeated per alternate allele. And the A100 is far from
saturated by a single window, so windows are **batched** — the campaign used `-b 128`. Deduplication and batching reduce repeated inference; the
throughput depends on the device, gene overlap and input composition.

### 2.4 Determinism

The campaign records settings that support deterministic execution in the configured environment:
`{{study.deterministic_runtime}}`. TF32 is disabled, and cuDNN autotuning is off to avoid
benchmark-driven algorithm selection. `torch.use_deterministic_algorithms(True)` requests deterministic
implementations and raises an error for known nondeterministic operations without an alternative.
These settings do not guarantee identical results across different hardware or library versions.

## 3. The scoring campaign

### 3.1 Partition and scale

The variant collection is partitioned into **{{study.source_chunks|,d}} chunks** with consecutive source records in each file. The analyzed subset has
{{primary.coverage.source_rows|,d}} source rows, which must not be confused with its gene-annotation count. A chunk is the atomic unit of scoring, auditing
and resume. Two independently seeded MANE checkpoints are scored (rs10 and rs13) as single models rather
than as the five-model ensemble SpliceAI publishes, which is a deliberate asymmetry discussed in
section 5.8.

### 3.2 Two hard limits that shaped the design

Neither limit is about the science, and both are invisible until you hit them.

- The GPU **QOS caps the account at ten GPUs**, shared across everyone using it.
- The controller's **`MaxJobCount` is about 100,000 array tasks in total**, with `MaxArraySize` 15,000.
  A one-task-per-chunk submission for a single seed is 100,000 tasks and therefore impossible; submitting
  past roughly 90,000 pending tasks simply returns `Resource temporarily unavailable`.

### 3.3 The submission design

Four choices follow from those limits.

- **Several chunks per task, scored sequentially.** Each array task scores its chunks one at a time on one
  GPU. Batched inference already saturates the device, so fanning out inside a task would only run out of
  memory. This keeps the task count comfortably inside both ceilings.
- **Idempotent at chunk granularity.** A chunk is skipped when its output already has the correct record
  count; a partial file from a killed task is detected by count mismatch, deleted and redone. Pausing and
  resuming costs only the handful of chunks in flight.
- **A whole-campaign GPU cap by construction.** SLURM's `%N` array throttle is *per array job*, so two
  seed arrays each throttled at four would use eight GPUs. Instead the seeds are **serialized into a
  dependency chain**, so only one array is ever runnable and the total concurrent GPU count is the
  throttle by construction rather than by convention.
- **Resume from a deep audit, not from filenames.** The resume path plans from a full audit of what is
  actually on disk, not from which files exist.

### 3.4 The audit

Before anything is analysed, every chunk is audited: exact byte size, nanosecond mtime, record count, a
canonical record digest, and raw SHA-256 of both the input and the output. The audit also verifies that
the output preserved the source — the seven fixed VCF columns and the original SpliceAI annotation must be
byte-identical after removing the added field — and that every added annotation is well-formed with its
predicted positions inside the allowed distance.

Provenance is graded rather than assumed. Outputs written after the scorer gained a cryptographic receipt
are `receipt_bound`; the outputs analysed here predate it and are `legacy_unprovenanced`, meaning they are
structurally audited and hash-pinned but their model identity is not cryptographically provable. The
reports say so rather than eliding it.

### 3.5 What went wrong: two incidents worth recording

**A packaging change silently emptied the campaign for days.** A routine `rm -rf build dist *.egg-info`
during a release deleted the editable install's metadata directory. `import openspliceai` continued to
work, because the path entry survived — so any check based on importing the package looked healthy — but
the **console script** could no longer resolve its distribution metadata and every task exited with
`PackageNotFoundError`, producing zero records. The lesson that generalised: a health check must exercise
the same entry point the work uses, not a convenient proxy for it.

**The campaign is currently stalled on allocation, not on error.** {{study.campaign_status}} The campaign handoff recorded `AssocGrpBillingMinutes` as the GPU scheduling reason;
this CPU analysis does not resume the GPU campaign. The
rs10 coverage was already sufficient for the analysis reported here, so the study proceeds and is labelled
provisional.

## 4. Analysing three billion paired annotations

### 4.1 Why not a table

{{primary.coverage.paired_annotations|,d}} paired annotations will not fit in memory, and materialising
them as an expanded analysis table would add substantial storage and I/O cost. Every statistic in this report is
therefore computed as an **additive counter**: sums, sums of squares and cross-products for the moment
statistics; fixed-width histograms for distributions; and explicit contingency tables for calls. A mapper
reduces a slice of chunks to those counters, and a reducer merges them. Nothing row-level survives except
a bounded deterministic sample and a bounded list of the largest discrepancies.

The cost of that choice is that rank and distribution statistics — Spearman, Kolmogorov–Smirnov,
Wasserstein, Jensen–Shannon — are computed from binned joint and marginal histograms and are **approximate**. They are marked
as such wherever they appear. Configured threshold counts are exact; moment statistics use floating-point sums,
and histogram-based transfer and difference tails retain their stated approximations.

### 4.2 The analysis unit

The unit is the tuple `(CHROM, POS, REF, ALT, gene)`, not a VCF row. A source row may carry annotations
for several overlapping genes, and a prediction may repeat an identical multi-gene annotation on every
row. The parser deduplicates identical repeats, counts and **excludes** conflicting values for the same
method and gene, and pairs only on an exact allele and an exact gene symbol. An annotation present on one
side and absent on the other stays in the coverage denominator and is never silently treated as a zero.

### 4.3 Fail-closed contracts

The pipeline refuses to produce a result it cannot stand behind. A reduction fails on a missing or
duplicated mapper task, a duplicated chunk, a pair list whose hash changed, mixed run labels or mixed
mapper configurations. A run may only be labelled **final** when its chunk domain is exactly the complete
one; everything short of that is **provisional**, and the report renderer refuses to describe a
provisional run as final. Every queued job re-verifies both the frozen input list and a recursive hash of
the analysis code before it runs, so code cannot drift between submission and execution.

### 4.4 What went wrong: two latent defects, found on first contact with the cluster

The analysis package was written, reviewed and unit-tested before it had ever been run on the scheduler.
Both of the following passed every test and failed every job.

**The code fingerprint could not be computed where it was checked.** Each job verified a recursive SHA-256
of the analysis package before running — and resolved the hashing script relative to `BASH_SOURCE`. SLURM
copies a batch script into a per-job spool directory, so that path did not exist at execution time and
every task exited on a check that was meant to protect it.

**The fingerprint was locale-dependent.** The digest sorted filenames without pinning collation, so the
submitting shell and the `--export=NONE` batch job hashed the same tree to different values. This did not
fail deterministically: a pilot submitted from the same shell passed because it happened to land on a node
whose locale matched, and the production arms then failed. Pinning `LC_ALL=C` inside the digest made it a
property of the tree rather than of the caller.

A third defect appeared only when the splice-site analysis was added: the reducer was assumed to be a pure
merger, when in fact it **rejoins the chunk-boundary groups the mappers defer**, which are real rows. It
therefore needed the same annotation the mappers used, and now verifies that it has exactly that one by
digest. A one-task pilot caught it before the production launch.

### 4.5 Independent verification

An independent standard-library implementation rereads the VCFs with its own parser,
grouping, conflict handling, three-way intersection, site derivation and arithmetic.
The primary window contains {{study.verification.primary.chunks|d}} contiguous chunks and
{{study.verification.primary.paired_annotations|,d}} paired observations; the matched window
contains {{study.verification.matched.chunks|d}} chunks and
{{study.verification.matched.paired_annotations|,d}} common observations.

The primary check is **{{study.verification.primary.status}}** across
{{study.verification.primary.checks|,d}} comparisons; the matched check is
**{{study.verification.matched.status}}** across {{study.verification.matched.checks|,d}}.
Integer counts, call tables, joint and marginal histograms, and DP counts must agree exactly.
The check also recomputes event statistics in every requested stratum. Continuous statistics
are compared with relative tolerance {{study.verification.relative_tolerance}} and absolute
tolerance {{study.verification.absolute_tolerance}}. The maximum observed absolute errors are
{{study.verification.primary.max_error}} and {{study.verification.matched.max_error}},
respectively. These are implementation checks on real windows, not independent biological
validation or an exhaustive reread of the full production domain.

The verification artifacts bind the requested inputs, annotation, comparison kind, verifier
configuration and compared summary digest. Cached independent data are accepted only if their
identity matches the requested run; absent expected comparisons cannot count as a passing check.

## 6. Discussion

The comparison separates several questions that are easy to conflate: numerical equality,
call overlap, agreement on the dominant event, and agreement on the selected position.
Shared zeros explain much of the apparent all-annotation agreement. The event-specific
statistics and signal-conditioned summaries reveal the difference hidden by that denominator.

The gain–loss contrast persists under the intended common masking, annotation, distance and
reference settings. Its cause is not isolated by this experiment. The two score collections
also differ in training, checkpoint identity, implementation and ensembling, and the published
comparator is rounded. The two-seed contrast provides a useful scale for comparison without
separating these causes.

Annotated-site proximity adds a way to describe where disagreement occurs. It does not resolve
whether lower gain scores reflect fewer false positives or more missed true sites. Evaluating
that distinction requires an independently measured splicing outcome, with a sampling scheme
that includes both shared and predictor-specific calls.

Threshold transfer should be guided by the application and independently validated. The
rate-matched and MCC-optimal cutoffs here describe agreement with SpliceAI on this population;
they are not calibrated clinical decision thresholds. Likewise, whether an OpenSpliceAI ensemble
would reduce the observed method gap remains an experiment, not a result of this study.

## 7. Limitations

- **Concordance only.** No experimental or clinical truth labels enter the analysis. Neither
  global agreement nor annotation proximity ranks biological accuracy.
- **Provisional coverage.** The primary arm contains {{primary.chunks|,d}} of
  {{study.source_chunks|,d}} source chunks. Incomplete outer groups are excluded. The matched
  arms cover {{study.rs13_genomic_extent}}, with an additional common-gene restriction.
- **Legacy provenance.** The scored files are structurally audited and hash-pinned but remain
  `legacy_unprovenanced`. The audit does not cryptographically establish the checkpoint that
  created them. Scoring settings and model labels are campaign records, not embedded proof.
- **Ensemble and seed scope.** The published comparator is an ensemble; each OpenSpliceAI arm is
  one checkpoint. Only two OpenSpliceAI seeds are compared. Architecture, training and ensemble
  contributions are not separately identified.
- **Annotation context.** The pooled internal-site index excludes transcript ends and differs
  from the per-gene mask. It gives neither a transcript-specific intronic annotation nor an
  experimental label. Variant distance and predicted-event distance are distinct quantities.
- **Quantization and approximations.** Distribution summaries use
  {{primary.histogram_binning.bins|d}} bins of width {{primary.histogram_binning.width|.3f}}.
  Epsilon-corrected floor binning prevents binary-edge underflow; empty bins can naturally
  reflect the comparator's decimal grid. Spearman and distribution distances are binned
  approximations, difference tails are rounded to bin centres, and transferred cutoffs are
  restricted to grid edges below one. Direct configured-threshold counts are exact; moment
  arithmetic has floating-point summation error.
- **Selection and dependence.** Top discrepancies are a selected extreme sample, signal subsets
  are conditioned on the scores, and adjacent variants and overlapping genes are dependent.
  Gene/block bootstrap intervals are resampling sensitivity summaries, not guarantees over
  independent biological observations. Large sample size does not remove these limitations.
- **Gene coverage.** Exact matching excludes unshared or conflicting gene annotations. The
  collapsed view changes the unit and can join signals assigned to different genes. Its
  agreement cannot be interpreted as recovering the missing exact-gene observations.
